# Analog and output-protection circuit (revision A)

The analog and protection sections are integrated into `design.yaml`. The source, generated schematic and exported connections have passed the static checks recorded in `circuit-baseline.md`; the headphone output is not electrically validated. The signal path is the ESS ES9039Q2M Figure 33 dual-op-amp stage (OPA1612) → stereo panel volume control → gain-2 OPA1622 stage → normally-open relay → 6.35 mm jack. `DAC_L_P/N` and `DAC_R_P/N` come from the DAC circuit. Independent schematic review checked the Figure 33 transcription against the ESS reference.

## Electrical target

The 4.0 Vrms target into 300 Ω is 13.33 mArms and 53.3 mW per channel; both channels together require 106.7 mW at the loads. This is an engineering test level, not a listening recommendation. A 2.0 Vrms DAC-stage full scale would need 2.00× voltage gain after the volume control. With ±9 V nominal rails, a 4 Vrms sine is 5.66 Vpeak, leaving 3.34 V nominal rail headroom before allowing for supply tolerance, output-stage swing, relay/contact and cable drops, and distortion. Verify this at low/high rail limits with both 300 Ω loads.

The ESS v0.2.3 Figure 33 reference is implemented per channel with one OPA1612 dual: lower section B has `+` at the DAC positive node, `−` at DAC negative, and two 3.6 kΩ feedback resistors in parallel plus 510 pF from DAC negative to B output. Upper section A has `+` at ground and `−` at the midpoint of two 4.7 kΩ resistors bridging A and B outputs, with 10 pF across the upper 4.7 kΩ. Both legs then use 140 Ω series and 2.2 nF to ground; the lower/B output is the single-ended signal and ground its return. The figure marks C24 and C25 open; they are omitted. Two dual OPA1612s provide stereo. The 0.47 µF local positive and negative rail bypass capacitors are included at each package; the OPA1622 driver also has local 0.47 µF bypass from each rail to ground. These are schematic choices; component/package selection and the resulting response still need simulation.

OPA1622 is a non-inverting gain-2 stage after the pot (10 kΩ feedback and 10 kΩ to ground per channel). That gain is conditional on 2 Vrms at the pot and remains to be reconciled with the actual DAC full-scale output. It is specified for ±2 V to ±18 V supplies and is capable of headphone-load currents well above this 18.9 mApeak 300 Ω target, but these datasheet capabilities do not prove the implemented circuit’s noise, stability, clipping, or thermal performance. Keep its feedback local and follow TI’s output-isolation / capacitive-load guidance after selecting the jack and PCB geometry. Use the exposed thermal pad tied to V− per the package datasheet and verified KiCad symbol pin 11.

## Hardware interlock and DC fault cutoff

`AUDIO_READY` is supplied by the DAC/control section and already incorporates upstream `PWR_GOOD`, DAC mute/format status, and its startup delay. A 74LVC2G00 cross-coupled NAND latch holds a detected DC fault until `AUDIO_READY` falls. A 74LVC1G08 generates `AMP_ENABLE = AUDIO_READY AND DC_OK`; its output drives OPA1622 `EN` and the relay-driver timing input. Both logic outputs have pulldowns so loss of 3V3_D leaves the amplifier disabled and relay open. Relay coil power comes from `V5_SYS`; do not derive it from USB VBUS.

For each channel, two 1 MΩ resistors average the pre-relay amplifier output with the shared 3V3_D divider reference. A 1 µF capacitor from the sense node to VREF filters output DC. Since VREF is loaded through both channel sense dividers, its nominal idle value is about 1.64 V and the sense baseline about 0.82 V; it is not a stiff 1.65 V source. The 21 kΩ and 48.1 kΩ threshold divider legs set approximately 0.573 V and 1.072 V, or about −0.50 V/+0.50 V output fault thresholds at nominal 3.3 V. Correctly oriented single BAT54WS-7-F clamps in SOD-323 limit LM339B input excursions; the 1 MΩ output-sense resistors bound estimated fault clamp current to about 11 µA for ±9 V output faults. Verify clamp forward voltage at that current and powered-off 3V3_D conditions against the LM339B absolute and recommended input limits.

The coupled VREF/sense capacitors have a startup excursion: independent review's nominal transient calculation found the sense outside the DC window for about 0.51 s with the earlier 100 kΩ/10 µF network. The current network is changed to 1 MΩ/1 µF sense filters and includes a DC_ARM RC plus 74LVC1G17; the selected 47 µF arm capacitor gives a multi-second nominal delay, but actual startup waveforms and worst-case capacitor derating/Schmitt thresholds have not been simulated. During arming the OPA1622 may turn on while the relay remains open. Once armed, DC_OK latches any sustained out-of-window result until disarmed; a second AND gates the relay path and a separate Schmitt/RC adds roughly 0.25–0.4 s close delay. A fault deasserts AMP_ENABLE and the relay-enable chain. Verify positive and negative faults on both channels, audio at 20 Hz and below, latch persistence, power loss, brownout, and relay reopening with dummy loads.

The Omron G6K-2F-Y coil is nominally 178 Ω ±10% at 5 V: 28.1 mA typical and 31.2 mA at minimum resistance (use at least 35 mA rail budget). Wire both normally-open poles in series with L/R; common-to-amp and NO-to-jack, leaving NC open. Drive the 5 V coil with a low-side transistor and a flyback diode (cathode to V5_SYS). BAS316 fast-discharge diodes use their SOD-323 footprint-compatible package; each has a 1 kΩ series resistor to limit discharge into the preceding logic output to about 3.3 mA initially at 3.3 V. A final 74LVC1G08 qualifies the coil driver with `RELAY_ENABLE AND RELAY_DELAYED`, so logic-low opens the relay immediately even while the timing capacitor is discharging. The intended relay-close delay is about 250 ms after `AUDIO_READY` and DC checks pass. The relay path is independent of the amplifier’s electronic shutdown.

## Validation gates

The configured OPA1622 4 Vrms bench uses two 2 Vrms inputs, ideal ±9 V rails, and two 300 Ω dummy loads, but it has not produced a valid result. A standalone single-channel test used TI's translated OPA1622 PSpice macromodel directly (no dual wrapper), ±9 V rails, a 10 kΩ/10 kΩ non-inverting gain-2 network, and a 300 Ω load. The command was `docker run --rm --network none --userns keep-id -v /tmp/opa1622-isolate:/test -w /test hwflow-audio:ca10fa8e timeout 20s ngspice -b op.cir`. It failed dynamic and true gmin stepping and source stepping, then stalled at “Transient op started”; the timeout returned exit 124 after 20.8 s, with no operating point or RMS measurement. Selecting KLU produced the same result. A dual-channel UIC/Gear transient had advanced only to roughly 1 µs at its one-minute observation. Host-level cleanup later found the full transient and UIC container jobs still running after roughly 18 and 15 minutes and stopped them; ending a tool wait had not terminated those containers. No valid result was obtained, so neither elapsed time is a successful simulation timing. These are solver/model convergence failures, not evidence that the amplifier does or does not meet the target. The 4 Vrms target remains unverified; this testbench also omits the ESS DAC stage, DC detector, latch, relay, and startup sequence. Before connecting headphones, verify DAC output limits; validate the macromodel or use another validated simulator; simulate startup/shutdown, ±DC faults, low-frequency audio, rail corners, and relay timing; inspect stability with actual output capacitance; then run ERC, DRC, and independent circuit review. No listening-level, acoustic safety, or production compliance claim is made.
