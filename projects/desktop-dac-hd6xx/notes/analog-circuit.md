# Analog and output-protection circuit (revision A)

The analog and protection sections are integrated into `design.yaml`. The source, generated schematic and exported connections have passed the static checks recorded in `circuit-baseline.md`; the headphone output is not electrically validated. The signal path is the ESS ES9039Q2M Figure 33 dual-op-amp stage (OPA1612) → stereo panel volume control → gain-2 OPA1622 stage → normally-open relay → 6.35 mm jack. `DAC_L_P/N` and `DAC_R_P/N` come from the DAC circuit. Independent schematic review checked the Figure 33 transcription against the ESS reference.

## Electrical target

The 4.0 Vrms target into 300 Ω is 13.33 mArms and 53.3 mW per channel; both channels together require 106.7 mW at the loads. This is an engineering test level, not a listening recommendation. A 2.0 Vrms DAC-stage full scale would need 2.00× voltage gain after the volume control. With ±9 V nominal rails, a 4 Vrms sine is 5.66 Vpeak, leaving 3.34 V nominal rail headroom before allowing for supply tolerance, output-stage swing, relay/contact and cable drops, and distortion. Verify this at low/high rail limits with both 300 Ω loads.

The ESS v0.2.3 Figure 33 reference is implemented per channel with one OPA1612 dual: lower section B has `+` at the DAC positive node, `−` at DAC negative, and two 3.6 kΩ feedback resistors in parallel plus 510 pF from DAC negative to B output. Upper section A has `+` at ground and `−` at the midpoint of two 4.7 kΩ resistors bridging A and B outputs, with 10 pF across the upper 4.7 kΩ. Both legs then use 140 Ω series and 2.2 nF to ground; the lower/B output is the single-ended signal and ground its return. The figure marks C24 and C25 open; they are omitted. Two dual OPA1612s provide stereo. The 0.47 µF local positive and negative rail bypass capacitors are included at each package; the OPA1622 driver also has local 0.47 µF bypass from each rail to ground. These decouplers now have 25 V ratings, clearing the 50% voltage-derating check at ±9 V. The source-derived analog-chain model results are recorded in model-repair.md.

OPA1622 is a non-inverting gain-2 stage after the pot (10 kΩ feedback and 10 kΩ to ground per channel). That gain is conditional on 2 Vrms at the pot and remains to be reconciled with the actual DAC full-scale output. It is specified for ±2 V to ±18 V supplies and is capable of headphone-load currents well above this 18.9 mApeak 300 Ω target, but these datasheet capabilities do not prove the implemented circuit’s noise, stability, clipping, or thermal performance. Keep its feedback local and follow TI’s output-isolation / capacitive-load guidance after selecting the jack and PCB geometry. Use the exposed thermal pad tied to V− per the package datasheet and verified KiCad symbol pin 11.

## Hardware interlock and DC fault cutoff

`AUDIO_READY` is supplied by the DAC/control section and already incorporates upstream `PWR_GOOD`, DAC mute/format status, and its startup delay. A 74LVC2G00 cross-coupled NAND latch holds a detected DC fault until `AUDIO_READY` falls. A 74LVC1G08 generates `AMP_ENABLE = AUDIO_READY AND DC_OK`; its output drives OPA1622 `EN` and the relay-driver timing input. Both logic outputs have pulldowns so loss of 3V3_D leaves the amplifier disabled and relay open. Relay coil power comes from `V5_SYS`; do not derive it from USB VBUS.

Each channel now uses two 100 kΩ resistors around a **33 µF capacitor to ground**, followed by a 49.9 kΩ/10 kΩ divider toward the shared reference. The comparator inputs remain positive while the analog rails are valid, including a clamped negative output fault. BAS116 low-leakage diodes clamp the filtered nodes; their verified local symbol maps anode 1, cathode 3 and NC 2 to SOT-23. The prior high-impedance Schottky-clamped circuit had unacceptable leakage sensitivity, and a capacitor to the moving reference could falsely trip on a bass transient.

U203 LM339B now operates from VPOS, where its input offset and bias specifications apply. Both window thresholds derive from the loaded reference, rather than assuming a stiff 1.65 V node. R251/R252 are 6.81 kΩ/100 kΩ; R253/R254 are 2.87 kΩ/100 kΩ. Exact threshold corners, leakage and capacitor assumptions are computed from the live source by `verification/protection_checks.py`; [protection-validation.md](protection-validation.md) is the current result, rather than the superseded nominal ±0.5 V estimate.

The arm network uses 120 kΩ and 100 µF with an SN74AUP1G17 buffer and low-leakage BAS116 discharge diode to allow the detector to settle. This conservative prototype has an approximately 18 s nominal startup delay; the qualified slow corner is about 37 s. That usability cost is explicit and can be revisited with measured capacitor/logic limits. Once armed, the real cross-coupled NAND topology latches a sustained fault until disarmed. The relay has an additional close delay, while `RELAY_ENABLE AND RELAY_DELAYED` removes its coil command immediately on fault or loss of audio readiness. R244 and R259 are now 10 kΩ: a 10 µA disabled-output leakage bound gives at most about 0.101 V instead of the previous possible 1 V. U204 uses the DCU ordering code matching its 0.5 mm VSSOP footprint; DCT was a different package.

The G6K-2F-Y 5 V coil is 237 Ω ±10%, about 21.1 mA nominal; the existing 35 mA supply allowance remains conservative. Common contacts connect to the amplifiers, normally-open contacts to the jack header, NC contacts are unused. D200 is BAS116 (anode pin 1 at DC_ARM_DISCHARGE, cathode pin 3 at AUDIO_READY, NC pin 2); D202 remains BAS316. Both discharge paths have 1 kΩ current limiting, and their different reverse-leakage bounds are included independently. The SS14 coil flyback diode changes release time: removing the coil command is not the same event as the contacts opening. A 20 ms release allowance in the behavioral checks is a prototype acceptance assumption, not an Omron guarantee with this diode.

## Validation gates

The repaired TI model passes the project amplifier bench and separate audio-band/headroom tests. The actual generated analog topology also passes with explicitly defined ideal DAC currents; its results do not establish real DAC full scale. See [model-repair.md](model-repair.md) for unchanged manufacturer models, parser settings, model limitations and the retained failed baseline.

Protection checks cover startup, positive/negative faults on both channels, latch persistence, rearm, full-scale 20 Hz signals, audio-ready loss and qualified power-state assumptions. The passive model is cross-checked against ngspice. Results and the precise unresolved bounds are in [protection-validation.md](protection-validation.md). Before connecting headphones, verify actual capacitor behavior, relay release with flyback, amplifier enable/mute transitions, shutdown/brownout transients and fault energy into dummy loads. Capacitive-load stability, noise/distortion and thermal behavior still need the physical layout and measurements.
