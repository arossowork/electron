
## Round 1
q1: No live and neutral, it only works on battery and it switches 220v actuators (thermal valve)
q2: Up to 60 minutes, 1 AA lasts about 13 months alkaline or 2 years lithium (recommended)
q3: 230 V thermal valve heads or zone valves, up to 2 A each (recommended)
q4: Both rooms are next to the box, cables shorter than 2 m

## Round 2
q1: Switch both outputs OFF and send a low-battery alarm to the app (recommended: no uncontrolled heating; rooms can get cold if the battery is not replaced)
q2: it is just a prototype, so cheap for small units (10 under 25$ each would be great)
q3: Plastic plates only (recommended: internal antenna, no extra cost)
q4: Cost first, then battery life, then size

## Round 3
q1: Low relays plus a screw-fixed battery cover, insulated probe tips, and an instruction to switch off the heating breaker before changing the battery (about +0.3 USD; good for the 10 prototypes, needs a safety review before CE)
q2: Thermal valve heads, or zone valve motors drawing under 1.5 A per output (recommended: the low relay is rated for this)
q3: Every 10 minutes (recommended: about 14 months on an alkaline AA, 2.3 years on lithium)

## Round 4
q1: Two AAA cells in series instead of one AA (recommended: half the current per cell, works down to 0 C until the cells are nearly empty, a bit more total energy, so about 1.5 years on alkaline, still to be confirmed; holder is wider but thinner)
q2: Use a higher-voltage, low-leakage capacitor (recommended: about +0.4 USD, keeps battery life on target)
q3: Yes, accept about 27-28 USD for the 10 prototypes (recommended: look for cheaper relays only for production)

## Round 5
q1: Keep 60 minutes and accept that a worst-case unit lasts about 11-13 months
q2: Accept the risk for the prototypes

## Round 6
q1: Add a 0.4 mm insulating sheet (polycarbonate or Formex, die-cut 62 x 40 mm) between the two boards (recommended: about 0.10 USD, stack goes from 28.2 to 28.6 mm of the 30 mm, standard practice for reinforced insulation through a solid layer)
